# Project Lumen Publication Standard

## Publication types

### Discovery brief

A short public explanation of a candidate finding. It must be labeled
preliminary and cannot claim novelty without expert review.

### Research note

A citable report containing the source, transcription, translation, historical
context, evidence, alternatives, bibliography, limitations, and review status.

### Digital edition

A sustained manuscript or text edition with editorial conventions, version
history, apparatus, and rights clearance.

### Dataset

Structured records with documentation, provenance, field definitions,
validation rules, and a license covering only material Project Lumen can
legally license.

### Scholarly article

A submission to a credible peer-reviewed venue. Public promotion must not
represent submission as acceptance.

## Required claim box

Every public research note begins with:

- **Claim**
- **Source**
- **Evidence**
- **Confidence**
- **What is uncertain**
- **Prior scholarship checked**
- **Expert review**
- **Rights status**
- **How to cite or correct this record**

## Headline rules

Forbidden without L5 status:

- “AI solves...”
- “Vatican secret revealed...”
- “Lost text discovered...”
- “Mystery finally decoded...”
- “Written by [person]” when attribution remains probabilistic.

Acceptable:

- “Project Lumen identifies a candidate...”
- “New transcription suggests...”
- “Marginal note may connect...”
- “Evidence strengthens the attribution to...”

## Corrections

Public records are versioned. Material corrections receive:

- a visible correction notice;
- date and reason;
- prior text retained in version history;
- changed confidence level;
- notification to collaborators or publishers when necessary.

## Independence

Project Lumen must not imply endorsement by the Vatican Library, Vatican
Apostolic Archive, Holy See, a university, or an adviser without written
authorization.
