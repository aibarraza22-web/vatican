# Donlan Evidence-Gap Map

Date opened: 13 August 2026  
Status: candidate research direction  
Current evidence level: L1

## Purpose

This workstream asks whether underpublished or untranslated Vatican records can
materially extend, complicate, or challenge Thomas A. Donlan's argument in *The
Reform of Zeal: François de Sales and Militant French Catholicism*.

This is separate from Investigation 001 concerning Cecco d'Ascoli. It does not
change Project Lumen's subject-neutral Grand Survey priorities.

## What is verified

1. Donlan's 2018 book studies François de Sales's Catholic *douceur*, or
   gentleness, as a critique of militant religious culture and as a source of
   nonviolent Catholic renewal. The University of St Andrews provides the full
   book and bibliographic record:
   <https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/17415>.
2. André Pératé's 1886 article published documents concerning François de
   Sales's Chablais mission from the Vatican archives. Pératé said that he
   selected from the voluminous correspondence of the Savoy nunciature,
   included roughly twenty then-unpublished letters of de Sales, and omitted
   many letters from the nuncio because he considered them repetitive:
   <https://doi.org/10.3406/mefr.1886.6491>.
3. The Vatican Apostolic Archive publishes an official description of Pier
   Paolo Piergentili's 1,150-page inventory of the Savoy nunciature archive,
   covering records from the sixteenth through eighteenth centuries:
   <https://www.archivioapostolicovaticano.va/content/aav/en/attivita/ricerca-e-conservazione/pubblicazioni/collane-editoriali---collectanea-archivi-vaticani--cav-/cav-97.html>.
4. The Vatican Apostolic Archive normally admits qualified scholars who hold at
   least a master's degree or equivalent and provide an appropriate letter of
   introduction. Project Lumen cannot assume physical access through Aiden or
   Codex:
   <https://www.archivioapostolicovaticano.va/content/aav/en/consultazione/accesso-e-consultazione.html>.

## What is not yet established

- No exact archival unit has yet been confirmed as unpublished.
- No exact document has yet been confirmed as never translated into English.
- Donlan's complete source base has not yet been compared with the modern Savoy
  nunciature inventory.
- Project Lumen has not established that Donlan omitted Vatican archival
  material. A bibliography census must test that question directly.
- The omitted nuncio letters may be repetitive and historically unimportant.
- The documents may have been used in later French, Italian, or German
  scholarship even if they have never appeared in English.

## Historical question

The central test is whether Roman and Savoyard records distinguish François de
Sales's personal theology and practice of gentleness from the political,
economic, or legal coercion surrounding the Chablais mission.

Candidate questions include:

- Did papal officials describe conversion as persuasion, political recovery,
  confessional conquest, or some mixture?
- Did they support or oppose confiscation, exile, restrictions, or other
  pressure against Protestants?
- Did de Sales resist coercion privately, accommodate it, or simply operate
  alongside it?
- Did Rome view *douceur* as a theological principle, a pastoral method, or a
  tactical instrument?
- Were conversion counts independently corroborated, politically motivated, or
  exaggerated?
- Did later canonization and institutional memory simplify a more conflicted
  historical record?

## Codex workflow

Codex should not begin by translating random pages. It should proceed in this
order:

1. Read Donlan's book for research purposes without committing the copyrighted
   PDF or DOCX to this repository.
2. Create a claim-and-source map that records every major argument, cited
   primary source, archival reference, unresolved question, and stated limit.
3. Read Pératé's article and list every published document, date, sender,
   recipient, old archival citation, editorial intervention, and explicit
   omission.
4. Map Pératé's nineteenth-century references to modern shelfmarks using the
   official Savoy nunciature inventory or a qualified researcher's help.
5. Search multilingual editions and scholarship for each exact document before
   assigning publication or translation status.
6. Rank exact candidate documents by relevance to Donlan, evidentiary value,
   feasibility, and novelty potential.
7. Only after an image or authorized reproduction is available, produce a
   diplomatic transcription, normalized original text, literal English
   translation, readable English translation, uncertainty log, and historical
   commentary.
8. Obtain review from Donlan or another qualified early-modern Catholic
   historian before advancing any claim to L4.

## Required source-census fields

Each candidate document should eventually record:

- modern repository and shelfmark;
- historical shelfmark used by Pératé or another editor;
- folio or page range;
- date, sender, recipient, place, and language;
- one-sentence content description;
- relationship to a specific claim in Donlan;
- original-language publication status;
- English-translation status;
- known scholarly discussions in every searched language;
- image or reproduction availability and rights;
- confidence, alternative interpretations, and falsification test.

## Stop conditions

Stop and report the blockage if:

- only a broad archival series can be identified, not an exact document;
- a source is behind an access barrier and no authorized copy is available;
- a modern publication already resolves the proposed question;
- the transcription cannot be anchored to a folio and image region;
- the rights status does not permit the intended use;
- a novelty claim depends only on an English-language web search.

## What Aiden will eventually need to do

Nothing is required for the initial source census. After exact shelfmarks and
research questions exist, Aiden should show the map to Thomas A. Donlan and ask:

1. Which candidate could materially extend or challenge *The Reform of Zeal*?
2. Which records has Donlan or another scholar already examined?
3. Would he review the historical framing and help obtain authorized archival
   access or reproductions?
