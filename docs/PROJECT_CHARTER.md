# Project Lumen Founding Charter

Version 0.1, 26 July 2026

## Purpose

Project Lumen is an independent, AI-assisted digital-humanities initiative
dedicated to discovering, translating, explaining, and responsibly publishing
historically valuable information in digitized Vatican and Vatican-related
manuscript collections.

The project begins with publicly accessible Vatican Library material. Work on
the Vatican Apostolic Archive requires its own credentials, permissions, and
institutional partnerships and is not implied by this launch.

## The opportunity

The central research problem is not a lack of images. It is the distance between
an image and a reliable historical conclusion. Manuscripts may be digitized
while remaining difficult to search because their text is handwritten,
abbreviated, multilingual, damaged, marginal, or described only at the volume
level.

Project Lumen attacks that gap with:

- systematic corpus reconnaissance;
- multiple independent transcription passes;
- language- and period-aware normalization;
- source-grounded translation;
- named-entity and network analysis;
- authorship, scribal-hand, and provenance hypotheses;
- structured uncertainty;
- human correction and external expert review.

## Long-term ambition

Within five years, Project Lumen should aim to become:

1. a respected open research index for under-described Vatican manuscripts;
2. a repeatable method for responsible AI-assisted historical discovery;
3. a network of students, historians, paleographers, linguists, technologists,
   and partner institutions;
4. a publisher of digital editions, datasets, research notes, and peer-reviewed
   scholarship;
5. a bridge between the Vatican's cultural patrimony and a global public.

## Founding research question

Which accessible digitized manuscripts have the highest chance of producing a
defensible contribution that materially changes historical knowledge?

## Initial campaign

The first campaign is called **The Lumen Grand Survey**. It is a subject-neutral
search across all accessible collections for the highest-impact unknowns.

Candidates receive priority when they could:

- recover text believed lost or unreadable;
- change the attribution of an influential text or decision;
- alter the accepted chronology of a major event, idea, institution, or
  discovery;
- reveal unknown communication among historically important actors;
- expose a meaningful difference between a draft and final text;
- document the transmission of a major idea across languages or regions;
- identify a previously unknown source used by an influential author;
- change the provenance, dating, or interpretation of an important manuscript.

The campaign does not assume that sensational-looking material is important.
Impact is judged by the size of the historical conclusion that could change,
the quality of the available evidence, and the degree to which the question
remains unresolved in scholarship.

## Workstreams

### Corpus intelligence

Build a metadata inventory, measure description depth, identify sparse or
anomalous records, and rank manuscripts for research value.

### Manuscript laboratory

Transcribe, normalize, translate, annotate, and compare selected pages. Preserve
every correction and disagreement.

### Historical intelligence

Connect people, places, dates, institutions, events, and texts. Test possible
authors, recipients, scribes, owners, and transmission routes.

### Verification

Run targeted bibliographic searches, falsification tests, and domain-expert
review. Record negative findings and rejected theories.

### Publication

Release research notes, digital editions, explainers, datasets, visual
networks, and papers. Never publish manuscript images without confirmed rights.

## Organization

### Founder and research director

The founder defines the mission, builds partnerships, sets priorities, and owns
the integrity of public claims.

### Scholarly advisory circle

The minimum viable advisory circle is:

- one paleographer matched to each selected script;
- historians matched to each selected question;
- one specialist in each language or corpus selected;
- one digital-humanities or machine-learning researcher;
- one rights and archival-practice adviser.

### Student research corps

Students may perform metadata work, preliminary transcription, bibliography,
and public explanation. Their work must enter the same review system as model
output.

## Ninety-day launch

### Days 1-14: Foundation

- Establish the project identity and evidence standard.
- Build the research ledger and manuscript-ranking model.
- Record Vatican Library image and publication restrictions.
- Identify 100 candidate manuscripts from high-potential collections.
- Prepare a one-page adviser invitation.

### Days 15-35: Reconnaissance

- Rank candidates by impact ceiling, under-description, novelty potential,
  evidentiary tractability, historical network density, and available
  comparison material.
- Exclude manuscripts already saturated by scholarship unless used as controls.
- Produce detailed dossiers on the top 20 candidates.
- Recruit at least two expert advisers before selecting the flagship corpus.

### Days 36-65: Pilot

- Select three manuscripts: one control, one feasible discovery candidate, and
  one high-risk/high-upside candidate.
- Process 150 pages, with at least 100 manually corrected.
- Measure transcription error by script, date, and model.
- Build an entity network and an evidence-backed hypothesis ledger.

### Days 66-90: First release

- Choose the strongest finding or methodological result.
- Conduct an adversarial novelty search.
- Obtain expert review.
- Publish Research Note No. 1 and a methods/data appendix.
- Publicly report errors, abandoned hypotheses, and remaining uncertainty.

## Impact strategy

Project Lumen becomes important through accumulated trust, not sensational
claims. Its strongest public identity is:

> ambitious enough to search for discoveries, disciplined enough to reject its
> own best stories.

Public communication should use high-quality storytelling while clearly
separating manuscript fact, scholarly interpretation, and unresolved
speculation.

## Measures of success

### Year one

- 5,000 manuscript records screened without subject-based preselection;
- 500 pages corrected;
- 10 research notes;
- 3 outside expert reviewers;
- 1 conference presentation or workshop;
- 1 submission to a credible journal or digital-humanities venue;
- documented permission for every republished image.

### Year three

- 25,000 records screened;
- 10,000 corrected pages;
- multilingual expert network;
- institutional partnership;
- peer-reviewed publications and reusable datasets;
- external researchers citing Project Lumen records.

### Year five

- recognized methodological standard for AI-assisted manuscript research;
- major collaborative editions;
- a pathway for qualified researchers to connect digital analysis with
  in-person archival investigation.

## Failure conditions

The project fails if it:

- treats translation fluency as proof of transcription accuracy;
- markets old scholarship as a new discovery;
- assigns authorship without comparison corpora and alternatives;
- republishes controlled images without permission;
- conceals model errors;
- prioritizes publicity over expert review;
- claims to represent or partner with the Vatican without written agreement.
